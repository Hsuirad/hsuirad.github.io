How an intersection was established, and what is machine-checked

Every claim carries a verbatim quote from both sources. Pass 2 of the verifier greps each quote back into the exact slide and the exact Bootcamp page it is attributed to, so a quote that was never on the page cannot survive. It also requires the tested value itself — the highlighted term — to appear on both sides; where the two sources word it differently, the claim must carry an explicit note saying so, and those notes are printed on the cards.

Pass 3 attacks the opposite failure. Passes 1 and 2 are blind to omission by construction: a document with one perfect claim passes both. So Pass 3 sweeps every substantive slide no claim cites, searches Bootcamp for it independently, and fails the build unless each one is either claimed or listed below with a reason. It also re-derives a random sample of claims from the claim text alone and checks the search returns the cited page.

What this does not prove: that a quote means what the claim says it means. That is a reading judgment. The quotes are printed on every card precisely so you can overrule it in seconds.
